A smaller trust surface

Less infrastructure to trust.

There is no account system and no directory. Optional supernodes help when your network needs them — they never hold your identity or the keys that seal room chat, files, and media.

From zero to connected

Trust starts with an invite.

No sign-up, no profile, no discovery service.

Create your identity

DoubleSlash generates an Ed25519 identity on your device and locks it with a local passphrase.

Share a signed invite

Send an https://doubleslash.space/i#… link. Everything after # carries the invite and stays out of the request to the website.

Connect

You connect directly when the network allows, or through a supernode you chose when it does not. Rooms always use a supernode.

What does the # in an invite link do?

The # is a fragment separator. Everything after it is the fragment identifier, often used as a page anchor. DoubleSlash uses it to carry the invite instead. Your browser requests https://doubleslash.space/i without sending the # or anything after it to the web server.

The landing page reads the invite locally in your browser and passes it to the installed DoubleSlash app, which validates it. Room invites use /r#… the same way. Keep the whole link, including everything after #, when you share or copy it. See how the handoff works →

Everything in one conversation

Voice, chat, files, and video share the same negotiated connection. If a direct call cannot connect within five seconds, it can fall back to a temporary private room on a supernode you already trust.

Rooms belong to the people in them

Up to 32 people per room. Definitions live on participant devices and come back when you reconnect. Public user-created rooms are off unless the operator turns them on.

The short answers

Frequently asked questions.

Deeper detail lives on the architecture page.

Can a supernode read my conversations?

No. Chat, voice, files, and video are sealed before they reach a supernode, and nodes never receive room keys. They do see routing metadata: peer and room IDs, membership, timing, byte volumes, room names, and file-offer details such as filename and size.

Do I need a supernode at all?

Direct one-to-one chat and calls do not, if the two peers can reach each other. A mutually trusted supernode can coordinate UDP hole punching, relay traffic, and host rooms. Rooms always need a supernode.

Can I make video calls yet?

On Windows, yes: camera, a screen or window, and shared audio. Linux and macOS can send camera video (VP8) but that path still needs hardware validation, and they do not capture screens or shared audio. Android can send camera video; incoming video is not wired yet. Voice, chat, rooms, and files work across the clients.

Is there an Android app?

Yes. Android uses the same Rust core through JNI, with Kotlin/Compose for chat, voice, files, rooms, portal access, backups, and camera sending. Incoming video and shared-audio playback are not connected yet. Desktop nightlies are the packaged client downloads; Android is built from the repository.

Can I use the games or pages in a normal browser?

No. They run inside the native client through d://, and are intentionally not exposed to the open web.

Was this called ConquerD?

Yes. The product is DoubleSlash. Invites people share are https://doubleslash.space/i#… links. The in-app portal uses d://. The landing page still hands an invite to an installed desktop client through doubleslash://.
